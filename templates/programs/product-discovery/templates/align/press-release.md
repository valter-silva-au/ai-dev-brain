# Press Release

<!-- One page. Not one page of small type — one page a reader finishes.
     Two rules do most of the work: write it as though the thing has already
     launched, and lead with the customer's benefit rather than the mechanism.
     If you cannot make it compelling in a page, the idea is not yet clear
     enough to build. That is the point of the exercise; a padded page hides
     exactly the finding you wanted. -->

**Date (future launch date):**

     Strong: "Teams can now move their existing data in without writing a
     script". -->

<!-- One sentence for the specific audience: who this is for and the benefit

## The problem

<!-- What was hard before, from the customer's point of view, in their language.
     No internal vocabulary, no reference to your architecture or your team's
     constraints.
     This should be recognisably the same problem as in the outcomes canvas. If
     it has drifted into something more impressive, the drift is the finding. -->

## The solution

<!-- How it works, briefly, and why it solves the problem stated above. Enough
     that a reader could explain it to someone else; not a feature list.
     Only include what is actually being built. A benefit that is aspirational,
     unfunded, or dependent on a later phase must not appear here as though it
     ships — a press release read as a commitment creates expectations the team
     never agreed to. -->

## Customer quote

<!-- What a real customer would say about the benefit, in the voice of a person

     unmarked is the single most damaging line in this document, because it
     looks like evidence and gets cited as evidence.
     A hypothetical quote you cannot imagine a customer actually saying is a
     signal the benefit is not real. -->
