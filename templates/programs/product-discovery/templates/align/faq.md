# FAQ

<!-- Two halves, and they are not the same document with different tone.
     The customer FAQ answers what a user would ask. The internal FAQ answers
     what a sceptical colleague would ask, including the questions with no good
     answer yet.
     The internal half is where this artifact earns its place. An FAQ made only
     of comfortable questions gets approved and then the real question arrives
     in the first review, unanswered and unowned. -->

## Customer FAQ

<!-- Written as a customer would ask, answered plainly. No internal
     terminology. If an honest answer is "not yet", say "not yet" and say what
     to do meanwhile — an evasive answer here becomes a support ticket. -->

### What is this and who is it for?

### What problem does it solve for me?

### How do I get started?

### What does it cost?

### What are the limitations I should know about before I start?

<!-- Do not skip this one. The limitation you omit is the one that generates
     the refund request. -->

### Does it work with <the thing they already use>?

### What happens to my existing data / setup?

### Where do I go if it does not work?

## Internal FAQ

<!-- The hard questions, asked in the sharpest form a colleague would use.
     Soften the question and you lose the whole point of the section.
     Where the answer is genuinely unknown, write "we do not know yet" plus who
     is finding out and by when. That is a credible answer. A confident
     non-answer is not. -->

### Why are we doing this now, rather than later or never?

### Why us, and what is the cost of not doing it?

### What is the appetite, and what gets cut first if we exceed it?

<!-- Must agree with the pitch. If it does not, one of the two documents is
     out of date and the disagreement is the finding. -->

### What are we explicitly not doing, and who will be unhappy about that?

### How will we know if this failed?

<!-- Answerable from the success metrics. If it is not, the metrics are not
     yet decision-grade. -->

### What is the riskiest assumption, and what happens if it is wrong?

### What could this break, and who owns the fallout?

### What is the ongoing cost after launch — support, operations, maintenance?

<!-- Frequently omitted, and it is the number that decides whether this is
     worth doing at all. -->

### Who has to approve this, and has anyone objected?

<!-- "No objections" is only credible if you can name who was asked. -->

### What did we consider and reject, and why?

### What happens if we do nothing?
