# Outcomes Canvas

<!-- One page. If it runs to two, you are describing a solution rather than
     framing an outcome. Fill top to bottom: customer, then problem, then
     what they do today, and only then what you propose. -->

## Customer segment

<!-- Who specifically. "Developers" and "our users" are not segments — they
     cannot be counted, interviewed, or excluded. Name a group narrow enough
     that you could list ten real members of it.
     Weak: "enterprise teams". Strong: "on-call engineers at teams running
     more than five services who currently triage alerts by hand". -->

## Problem

<!-- The top one to three problems this segment has, stated as their problem,
     not as the absence of your feature. "They have no dashboard" is your
     solution inverted; "they cannot tell which of five services caused the
     page" is their problem.
     Note how you know. If the answer is "we assume", write "assumed" here —
     an honest assumption beats a fake finding. -->

## Existing alternatives

<!-- What the segment does about this problem TODAY, including doing nothing,
     a spreadsheet, or a competitor. There is always an alternative; if you
     cannot name one, the problem may not be painful enough to act on.
     This is also your real bar: your solution has to beat this, not beat
     nothing. -->

## Proposed solution

<!-- Two or three sentences. Enough to distinguish it from the alternatives,
     not enough to constitute a design. Detail belongs in the pitch.
     If this section is the longest one on the page, the canvas has inverted:
     you are solution-first and the problem is being retrofitted. -->

## Value proposition

<!-- One sentence: for <segment> who <problem>, this <does what> so that
     <outcome they care about>. The outcome must be theirs, not yours —
     "so that we increase adoption" is your goal, not their value. -->

## Baseline metric

<!-- What is true today, with a number, a source, and a date. This is the
     single most abused box on the canvas: a baseline you cannot point to a
     query, report or count for is not a baseline.
     If it is genuinely unmeasured today, write "not currently measured" and
     say what it would take to measure it. That is a real finding. -->

| Metric | Target | Timeframe | Commitment or hypothesis? |

## Timeframe

<!-- When the target is expected to be reached and when you will check. Name
     the checkpoint date, not just the end date — a target only disciplines
     the work if someone looks at it before it is too late to change course. -->

## Out of scope

<!-- What this explicitly does not address, especially adjacent problems that
     stakeholders will assume are included. Omitting this box is how scope
     arrives later disguised as a misunderstanding. -->

## Open questions

<!-- What you would need to know to raise your confidence, and who could
     answer it. Empty here almost always means unexamined, not certain. -->
